# Style guide: banned phrases, template, finance/law framing

## Banned openers (story-like — the top failure)

Never start an answer with any of these:

- "Imagine..." / "Picture this..." / "Picture yourself..."
- "Say you're..." / "Let's say..." / "Suppose..."
- "You wake up..." / "You just found out..."
- "Meet [name]..." (a made-up character)
- "Ever wonder...?" / "Have you ever...?"
- "So, what happens when...?" / "What if...?"
- "Once..." / "It was..."
- "Here's the thing..." / "The truth is..." / "Let's be honest..."
- "Now," as a scene-shift opener.

Start with the answer instead.

## Banned throughout (figures of speech and hype)

double-edged sword, perfect storm, tip of the iceberg, navigate the waters,
ticking clock, safety net, kick the can down the road, moving the goalposts,
at the end of the day, the bottom line, level playing field, low-hanging fruit,
think outside the box, game-changer, crucial, vital, powerful, seamless,
robust, unlock, supercharge, deep dive, when it comes to, needless to say.

## Banned punctuation habits

- No exclamation marks.
- Few em-dashes; a comma or a full stop usually works.
- No "..." for suspense.
- No dramatic one-word or two-word sentences.

## Word swaps (short over long, plain over jargon)

use ← utilize/leverage · buy ← purchase · about ← approximately ·
before ← prior to · after ← subsequent to · start ← commence · end ← terminate ·
enough ← sufficient · more ← additional · help ← facilitate/assist ·
show ← demonstrate/indicate · many ← numerous · need ← require · get ← obtain ·
because ← due to the fact that · to ← in order to · if ← in the event that ·
now ← at this point in time · some ← a number of · encourage ← incentivize.

## Direct-answer template

> [Sentence 1: the answer, stated flat.] [Sentence 2: the main reason or rule.]
> [Sentence 3-4: the key facts and numbers a person needs to act.] [Optional:
> the one exception that changes the answer.] [Last sentence: the caveat or the
> next step that matters most.]

Five sentences or more. Active voice. No opener before the answer.

## Finance/law framing

- Answer the question the person asked, not a nearby one.
- Give the rule, then the number, then the exception.
- Name who does the thing: the IRS, the court, the lender, the executor.
- Flag when the answer depends on facts you do not have (state, filing status,
  date of death, dollar amounts).
- Do not give it as personal advice; give the rule and the usual result, and say
  when to check with a pro. Keep that caveat to one plain sentence.

## Worked before/after

Before (story-like, breaks rules):
> Imagine you've just inherited your dad's IRA — it can feel like a windfall, but
> there's a ticking clock. The money is taxed by the IRS, and needless to say,
> you'll want to navigate these waters carefully.

After (direct, plain):
> You do not have to take the money out right away, but you usually have ten
> years to empty the account. Each dollar you withdraw counts as ordinary income
> that year, so the IRS taxes it at your normal rate. Spreading the withdrawals
> across the ten years keeps any single year's tax bill lower.
