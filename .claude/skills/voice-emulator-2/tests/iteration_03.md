No, you do not have to take the money out right away, but as your dad's child you usually must empty the account within ten years of his death. Every dollar you pull from a traditional IRA counts as ordinary income, so the IRS taxes it at your normal rate that year. If your dad was already old enough to take yearly minimum withdrawals before he died, you also have to take a small amount each year in years one through nine. You will not owe the ten percent early-withdrawal penalty on this money, whatever your age, because inherited IRAs are exempt from it. Spreading your withdrawals across the full ten years keeps any single year's tax bill lower. The rules differ for a spouse or a few other heirs, so check your own case with a tax pro before you act.
